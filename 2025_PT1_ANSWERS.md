## Module 1 explanations

### Question 1: Choice B, `speculates`

DB question ID: `f2a90205-1147-591e-be71-dc62bc9021d5`

Ochoa lacks a definite timeline, and the passage calls her prediction a "conjecture." Therefore, she **speculates**, meaning that she proposes an idea without firm proof. She neither demands nor establishes that outcome, and she does not doubt it.

### Question 2: Choice C, `persistent`

DB question ID: `f329f98f-5a27-54db-9509-10c07107a223`

Wauneka worked continuously for public health over many years, so **persistent** precisely describes the sustained effort. The effort is not characterized as impartial, casual or offhand, or legally mandatory.

### Question 3: Choice D, `exemplifies`

DB question ID: `f0254212-c96d-5904-9969-e8b069c5eed8`

The collaboration puts the community-based participatory model into practice: tribal citizens and scientists jointly designed and conducted the research. It therefore **exemplifies** the model rather than avoiding, overshadowing, or inventing it.

### Question 4: Choice A, `synchronization`

DB question ID: `6c364259-d77e-55b9-9ea7-0503eeac9aa0`

The dodder and its host flower at the same time, which is **synchronization**. The protein transfer explains their matching timing; it does not describe dormancy, prediction, or a reduction in intensity.

### Question 5: Choice B, `a straightforward`

DB question ID: `7473aa4c-fc35-5722-970c-352ee69d1951`

Planet formation in a binary system seems nearly impossible and requires a complex combination of supporting factors. Thus, the phenomenon has lacked **a straightforward**, or simple and direct, explanation. The issue is complexity, not bias, perceptibility, or a shortage of inconclusive proposals.

### Question 6: Choice A, `repudiates`

DB question ID: `9fed546b-f8e3-598c-b3e6-127758055e75`

Harjo rejects television's habit of placing Native people only in the distant past by depicting contemporary teenagers. **Repudiates** means rejects and matches the passage's explicit reference to this rejection. The other verbs mean announces, predicts, or withdraws one's own earlier claim.

### Question 7: Choice A

DB question ID: `43650a2f-dd6f-5f4f-9f72-7d9dd58db325`

The passage traces how von Ahn's book-digitization problem led him to invent reCAPTCHA and explains how the test worked. Its main purpose is therefore to discuss that invention, not scanner mechanics, the digitization project by itself, or reCAPTCHA's popularity.

### Question 8: Choice D

DB question ID: `36a1becd-1131-5f4b-910c-583629ec435e`

The first sentence says Lily responds strongly when a setting reflects her feelings. The underlined sentence illustrates that idea by showing her identify the landscape's calmness and breadth with her present mood. The following sentence merely supplies visual details rather than expanding the emotional claim.

### Question 9: Choice C

DB question ID: `560c146c-8df6-5dff-a452-b95c0dcd09a8`

The underlined sentence identifies the data and the comparison used in the study: forecasts were compared with actual earnings. It therefore explains part of the **methodology**. It does not report results, give a specific example, or identify a research obstacle.

### Question 10: Choice C

DB question ID: `b2bdc200-5c03-52b6-8936-ab0ac46951d2`

The passage directly says Mother writes stories and makes up poems for her children. It does not say that she wants more social visits, prefers birthdays to other occasions, or ranks reading above her other activities.

### Question 11: Choice A

DB question ID: `c702e749-0c57-51fd-bdb7-3dfafa5c367e`

The speaker praises Dunbar for understanding both people (their "hearts and souls") and nature (flowers and the brook). The poem is not about literal extensive reading, a detailed writing process, or memories of an afternoon together.

### Question 12: Choice A

DB question ID: `14353210-1a28-594b-9202-99f4a1f847e5`

Choice A directly tells readers, "You have not known what you are," then develops the idea through images of sleep and closed eyes. The other quotations describe readers' vastness, the speaker's regret, or the speaker's understanding, not readers' failure to understand themselves.

### Question 13: Choice A

DB question ID: `49d3865c-9304-5424-86ea-6dbdaeb47372`

Careful photographs of Indigenous communities, customs, and places would serve as records of cultures and directly support the claimed ethnographic value. Technical skill, international publication, and the prior popularity of the subjects do not establish that value.

### Question 14: Choice C

DB question ID: `3c698d25-f2c2-5d64-8638-7fbdd5058a89`

The table gives 66 acting credits for Lillian St. Cyr and 58 directing credits for Edwin Carewe. If records were lost, both actual totals could be higher, exactly illustrating why the listed figures are minimums. The other choices merely compare totals or contradict the table's dates and counts.

### Question 15: Choice D

DB question ID: `132a3994-9a7d-5ff1-a76d-b50684599a5a`

Only about 15% of plants would occur in vegetation patches under random distribution, yet more than half of every species' juveniles were found there. That substantial overrepresentation supports an early survival advantage from growing near other plants. The other statements either omit the random baseline or misstate the data.

### Question 16: Choice C

DB question ID: `8e9d8743-a1c2-5119-a323-ec47cc6222bd`

If the roots dissolve new channels even when ready-made cracks are available, channel creation alone cannot fully explain the behavior; obtaining phosphate is a plausible additional benefit. Different acid proportions are irrelevant, and thriving on phosphate-free rock would weaken the proposed nutrient mechanism.

### Question 17: Choice B

DB question ID: `78e3eaf8-8aa0-5249-916a-029bdd2da2a8`

Major increases in sauropod size did not coincide with spikes in atmospheric carbon dioxide. The evidence therefore undermines the claim that increased carbon dioxide was necessary for gigantism. The passage supplies no basis for lineage-specific effects, a higher later level, or harm from slightly higher carbon dioxide.

### Question 18: Choice B

DB question ID: `832f865e-50bf-5059-a690-ea5b1aaae522`

Allen says strong opinions anticipate and rebut objections. Discussing philosophers who disagree would help judges test and improve their arguments. It would not eliminate research, guarantee accessibility to nonexperts, or align rulings with philosophers' majority views.

### Question 19: Choice A, `they`

DB question ID: `c9c45c7a-23ef-5361-9631-f44fb5027abe`

The antecedent **customers** is plural, so the matching third-person plural pronoun is **they**. `One` and `it` are singular, while `you` improperly shifts to second person.

### Question 20: Choice A, `soul," positing`

DB question ID: `a8155739-ffc8-5dbc-b091-b35b4c5c42a5`

The main clause ends with the quotation, and **positing** begins a participial phrase that explains Epicurus's definition, so a comma correctly marks the boundary. A colon or semicolon cannot introduce that dependent phrase, while a period would leave `Positing ...` as a fragment.

### Question 21: Choice C, `Their`

DB question ID: `71ee5e44-6fc2-5a72-b7f0-ddbd7bf6fd08`

The findings belong to the plural pair Watson and Crick, so the plural possessive determiner **Their** is required. `They're` means `they are`, `it's` means `it is` or `it has`, and `its` is singular.

### Question 22: Choice C

DB question ID: `9d1564f1-80b6-59a6-b7c8-36608b1b4301`

`Stina Chyn` is essential because it identifies which critic is meant, so no commas should set off the name. The quotation is grammatically integrated as the object of `claims`, so no comma is needed before it either.

### Question 23: Choice B, `claim`

DB question ID: `b90e88b3-bbfd-5969-bf08-d596971e29d2`

The subject `some historians` needs a finite main verb, and **claim** supplies the correct present-tense plural form. `Claiming`, `having claimed`, and `to claim` are nonfinite and would leave the clause without its main verb.

### Question 24: Choice A, `food:`

DB question ID: `b78ca8c1-5f18-539f-9d0e-4986b227d147`

The first independent clause announces that researchers know why the worms move oppositely, and the material after the blank explains why. A colon correctly introduces that explanation. A comma creates a splice, while no punctuation creates a run-on; `while` does not express the needed explanatory relationship.

### Question 25: Choice B, `themselves`

DB question ID: `9aa4ed3c-d6fb-529d-ad6b-7add0f4b2ed1`

The plural subject `turtle barnacles` performs the attaching action on itself, requiring the plural reflexive pronoun **themselves**. The singular forms do not agree, and `them` is not reflexive.

### Question 26: Choice A, `allows`

DB question ID: `1f2db57b-7146-5154-be19-af150b7eb3c1`

The grammatical subject is the singular gerund **landing**, so the singular verb **allows** agrees with it. The other choices use plural verb forms.

### Question 27: Choice A, `equations, though:`

DB question ID: `e6729be4-0d95-56df-b190-90ae48000f16`

The comma sets off the supplementary adverb **though**, and the colon introduces the explanation of how Hopper's career extended beyond equations. A comma alone would splice two independent clauses, a period before `Though` creates an illogical contrast, and no punctuation creates a run-on.

### Question 28: Choice A

DB question ID: `52742deb-b6f3-510a-b556-a8537a010641`

The introductory modifier `Upon recovering two years later` must immediately modify the person who recovered: **Henry**. The other constructions make a reign or an unclear `it` appear to recover, producing dangling modifiers.

### Question 29: Choice D, `for example,`

DB question ID: `f43f88d1-9694-5a46-a1b8-4a30a822adac`

*The Emperor's Babe* is a specific instance of the previously described hybrid works, so **for example** states the relationship. It is neither a contrast nor a consequence, and `secondly` incorrectly treats it as a separate point.

### Question 30: Choice C, `by contrast,`

DB question ID: `8962bc70-d888-5c2c-b695-0034f19c8fea`

At two weeks, wolves can smell but cannot see or hear, whereas dogs have all three senses. **By contrast** correctly marks this opposition. The second statement neither restates nor exemplifies the first and is not its consequence.

### Question 31: Choice D, `Increasingly,`

DB question ID: `7e608c81-3d75-5c8b-ad0c-e911ba832400`

The passage describes a shift from traditionally solitary work toward more collaboration. **Increasingly** expresses that growing trend. The sentence elaborates on the shift rather than adding a separate similar point, giving a cause, or merely adding information.

### Question 32: Choice D

DB question ID: `4893859b-ac36-516f-a15c-431fea82118e`

Choice D presents both the study and its finding: Chinsamy-Turan compared growth lines and concluded that the jawbones were juvenile. The other choices omit the conclusion, the researcher, or the finding.

### Question 33: Choice C

DB question ID: `b960ece7-357f-54e5-bdd4-e530f6105b5e`

Choice C directly compares the women's distinct contributions: Hedgeman secured a place for a woman speaker, while Bates delivered the speech. The other choices omit one contribution or provide background without making the requested comparison.

## Module 2 explanations

### Question 1: Choice B, `observant`

DB question ID: `ed2d0d08-26fd-5e7e-8452-d18d674d68bd`

Lawrence's close attention to neighborhood details made him **observant**, meaning watchful and perceptive. The passage does not describe doubt, fault-finding, or self-assurance.

### Question 2: Choice D, `a substantial`

DB question ID: `50726a3f-5019-54ba-b040-6a9a5f7fc616`

The researchers are testing for `considerable variations` in venom potency, so they are asking whether age and sex have **a substantial**, or sizable, effect. The issue is magnitude, not whether the effect is disturbing, acceptable, or imperceptible.

### Question 3: Choice A, `involuntarily`

DB question ID: `6d609a57-e6f9-5f1f-8970-084aab2ea06f`

The next sentence calls the contractions uncontrollable, so **involuntarily** precisely means that they occur without conscious control. The other options describe usefulness, effort, or smoothness rather than lack of control.

### Question 4: Choice D, `intersect`

DB question ID: `cac7685f-73b1-5ee2-b91d-1557f6c5327f`

Okuma's work is both museum art and purchasable fashion, challenging the belief that those fields rarely **intersect**, or overlap. `Prevail`, `succumb`, and `diverge` do not describe the claimed lack of overlap.

### Question 5: Choice D, `prescribed`

DB question ID: `9cfa8e17-146e-597d-b31c-0aba97fa46ad`

The expectations are socially dictated rules, so they are **prescribed** by Elizabethan society. The other verbs mean inserted, carried out or entrusted, and depicted, none of which fits rigid social expectations.

### Question 6: Choice B, `innocuous`

DB question ID: `d3d2e1a1-6ac4-5d63-92ac-074b49967ac9`

The vibration and warming are explicitly harmless, making **innocuous** the precise description. The contrast is with a less benign shock, not with deceptive, new, or unusable treatments.

### Question 7: Choice D, `peripheral`

DB question ID: `fc97c443-f422-564f-8b4a-8e4fe7dab33c`

Xoconochco lay hundreds of kilometers from the capital, near the empire's outer edge, so its location was **peripheral**. Distance from the center does not mean hidden, unobtrusive, or imprecisely known.

### Question 8: Choice A

DB question ID: `4ec6909d-f6ab-50c7-9d65-00a6c2bfc0b4`

Jane outwardly conducts her day calmly while inwardly and repeatedly imagining reasons and advertisements for leaving. The passage therefore contrasts outward calm with internal restlessness; it does not establish loyalty, fulfillment, or a final decision to leave.

### Question 9: Choice B

DB question ID: `55c01fe3-5c47-53d8-9b10-c838fa045d55`

Text 1 calls the shared EGR gene especially promising. Text 2 stresses that its human function is unknown and probably differs from its function in worms, so Text 2 would regard Text 1's enthusiasm as **overly optimistic**.

### Question 10: Choice D

DB question ID: `231ad14a-243c-51aa-9057-ff3d9c3e0e28`

Although tired and in bed, the speaker stays awake because thoughts travel mentally to the distant friend. The journey is figurative, not an actual or planned trip, and the speaker is neither asleep nor conversing with the friend.

### Question 11: Choice D

DB question ID: `2b4341b3-61a4-5877-9b66-56bd6fbce480`

The Lord Chancellor calls the shouting unanimous even though people yell different demands and do not know what they want. He thus describes unity where the scene plainly shows confusion and disagreement.

### Question 12: Choice A

DB question ID: `eb662eac-ff42-5eec-821e-38e03704d0fe`

Choice A directly links Alexandra's emotions to the country through music-like insects, her heart in the grass, and a sense of the future in the ridges. The other quotations concern practical learning, travel, or business skill rather than emotional attachment to nature.

### Question 13: Choice B

DB question ID: `6f8e34d0-077f-5e2a-8ead-36f1d1287c35`

Vietnamese is spoken much more slowly than Spanish (about 5.3 versus 7.7 syllables per second) but conveys information at nearly the same rate (about 42.5 versus 42.0 bits per second). That directly supports the claim that different speech speeds can yield similar information rates.

### Question 14: Choice A

DB question ID: `b1ac0532-ac4a-5958-a9ac-8fb74f6c2d7e`

Participants who viewed the awe-inspiring trees and then picked up significantly more spilled pens would demonstrate increased helpful behavior after awe. Positive descriptions and mere noticing do not establish altruism, while greater awe among nonhelpers would weaken the claim.

### Question 15: Choice A

DB question ID: `72e9526b-5e19-594a-9216-3a3cd89ccdd1`

From 1900 to 1950, France's agricultural share fell 11 points and its service share rose 7, while the US changes were much larger: down 27 and up 22. This interval most clearly demonstrates different transition rates; the other intervals show broadly similar long-run changes.

### Question 16: Choice C

DB question ID: `2386531b-ccf7-5be6-b461-57a431ab859f`

If automated categorization frees archaeologists to perform important work requiring human expertise, the model supplements rather than replaces them. Greater model speed would strengthen replacement concerns, while shared errors or limited training do not show that archaeologists retain uniquely human tasks.

### Question 17: Choice A

DB question ID: `654e235b-88a2-5329-9d01-13ffdd780b95`

Veterans already know organizational structures similar to civilian government bureaucracy, so those familiar workplaces may be especially appealing. The passage does not discuss nonveterans, new job requirements, or an atypical change in veterans' career preferences.

### Question 18: Choice D

DB question ID: `4482bb9a-d101-5e2e-af9b-540ed9cc3cd2`

Some tanagers can look richly colored through light-manipulating feather structures without consuming the carotenoids associated with health. Their appearance can therefore falsely imply high fitness, making it a **dishonest signal**.

### Question 19: Choice C, `has been`

DB question ID: `612b6e1d-114b-5bc7-81b4-7b297af205b2`

The subject is the singular mass noun **writing**, so the singular verb phrase **has been** agrees with it. The other choices use plural verb forms.

### Question 20: Choice B, `out, but`

DB question ID: `d365dda0-7abf-52e0-9a9e-d28057413bf1`

The sentence contains two independent clauses joined by the coordinating conjunction **but**, so a comma must precede the conjunction. Omitting the comma is incorrect for these full clauses, while omitting `but` creates either a run-on or a comma splice.

### Question 21: Choice C, `forcing`

DB question ID: `219b0741-4f45-5bf9-bdbf-a348262b73e7`

The present participle **forcing** begins a phrase that supplements the main clause by describing the consequence of unavailable food. Finite forms cannot fill that role, and `to force` incorrectly suggests purpose.

### Question 22: Choice D, `could the blueberries thrive?`

DB question ID: `92356f57-c95b-5e58-865d-b372ff0e4602`

Michel is asking a direct question, so English requires interrogative word order (**could the blueberries thrive**) and a question mark. Choices A and B use the wrong ending punctuation, while C uses declarative word order for a direct question.

### Question 23: Choice D, `is`

DB question ID: `79c8df4e-6fd2-5534-b45b-a63c60d91e4d`

The time marker **Today** requires the present-tense **is** to describe Paik's current status. Future, past perfect, and simple past forms do not express that current judgment.

### Question 24: Choice C, `adjustments. Prior`

DB question ID: `cc1a64b4-d134-5a58-8760-310e316069d4`

The first sentence ends after `adjustments`; **Prior to VisiCalc's release** begins a new sentence explaining the earlier process. A period correctly separates the independent sentences. The other choices fuse or splice them or misuse `and`.

### Question 25: Choice B, `create`

DB question ID: `b3579b65-42dc-58d3-be9c-d0ed7ec4df56`

The modal **would** governs both coordinated verbs: `would increase ... and create`. Therefore, the base form **create** is required; finite, participial, and past-tense alternatives do not fit the parallel construction.

### Question 26: Choice D

DB question ID: `f8e18d37-e549-5118-8e87-d19f2db77af8`

The introductory modifier `Despite being cheap, versatile, and easy to produce` must immediately modify **commercial plastics**. Choice D places that noun directly after the modifier and then introduces the two problems with a colon. The other choices make `there` or the problems themselves appear cheap and versatile.

### Question 27: Choice C

DB question ID: `7ffb48b1-1198-57af-bec0-7db37329ea47`

`Yuree Lee` is essential information identifying which plant cell biologist wrote the article, so the title and name take no separating commas. Every other choice inserts at least one unnecessary comma.

### Question 28: Choice D, `As a result,`

DB question ID: `d0554e84-c5d8-5b57-a3ae-848c5dc01944`

Spatulae bond temporarily with touched atoms; **as a result**, spiders can cling to surfaces. The final statement is a consequence, not an example, contrast, or merely similar fact.

### Question 29: Choice A, `Still,`

DB question ID: `96bb620d-495d-5737-8982-b069a0b80f34`

Paris appears ideal for Sher-Gil's artistic development, yet she longs to return to India. **Still** correctly signals this unexpected contrast. The desire is not presented as a result, confirmation, or additional supporting detail.

### Question 30: Choice C, `Similarly,`

DB question ID: `52463754-5be1-556b-9c93-721ba5c6673a`

The Los Angeles tea campaign is parallel to the San Francisco campaign, and both exemplify the broader statewide strategy. **Similarly** links the two equivalent examples; the second neither restates nor concludes the first.

### Question 31: Choice B

DB question ID: `64e77b83-02fd-5b81-9f9e-28f7a6fde0e4`

Choice B directly compares both lengths, stating that the roughly 33-mile Seikan Tunnel is slightly longer than the approximately 31-mile Channel Tunnel. The other choices mention only one length, generalize, or identify locations without comparing lengths.

### Question 32: Choice B

DB question ID: `9f850adb-dd98-5d34-9af9-4a809a19d0ef`

Choice B both defines **flauna** as Ching's plant-animal hybrids and gives a concrete example, a parrot with leaves for feathers. The other choices provide only a partial definition, titles without a specific example, or examples without a full explanation.

### Question 33: Choice D

DB question ID: `06421d60-3c19-5c9f-82e0-5a48f40d6dc6`

Calling Taylor the **only** Black woman to publish a Civil War memoir directly emphasizes the uniqueness of her accomplishment. The other choices describe meaningful events or the book's availability but do not establish that her achievement was one of a kind.
