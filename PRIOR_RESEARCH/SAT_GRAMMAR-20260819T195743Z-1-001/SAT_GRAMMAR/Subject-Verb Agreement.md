# Subject-Verb Agreement

> **Core Rule:** The verb in a sentence must agree in number with its subject. A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.

**The #1 SAT strategy:** Identify the true subject — ignore everything between the subject and the verb, especially [[Prepositions and Prepositional Verbs|prepositional phrases]].

---

## Basic Agreement

| Subject Type | Example | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | The dog **runs** across the yard. | One dog → singular verb |
| Plural | The dogs **run** across the yard. | Multiple dogs → plural verb |
| Tricky phrase in between | The box of chocolates **is** on the table. | "Box" is the subject, not "chocolates" |

---

## Tricky Cases the SAT Loves

### Prepositional Phrase Interrupts Subject and Verb

The collection of rare stamps **was** donated to the museum.
*(Not "were" — "collection" is singular.)*

See [[Prepositions and Prepositional Verbs]] for more on how these phrases create distractors.

### Inverted Sentence Order (verb before subject)

Among the ruins **stands** a single tower.
*(Flip it: "A single tower stands among the ruins.")*

### Compound Subjects Joined by "and"

Peanut butter **and** jelly **are** a classic combination.
*(Two things = plural.)*

### Compound Subjects Joined by "or" / "nor"

Neither the teacher **nor** the students **were** prepared.
*(Verb agrees with the **closer** subject — "students" is plural.)*

### Indefinite [[Pronouns]] — Always Singular

Each, every, everyone, everybody, everything, anyone, anybody, anything, someone, somebody, something, no one, nobody, nothing, either, neither

> Each of the students **has** a textbook.
> Neither of the options **is** appealing.

### Indefinite [[Pronouns]] — Always Plural

Both, few, many, several

> Few of the applicants **were** qualified.

### Indefinite [[Pronouns]] — Either Singular or Plural

Some, all, none, any, most — depends on the object of "of"

> Some of the cake **is** gone. *(cake = singular)*
> Some of the cookies **are** gone. *(cookies = plural)*

---

## 💡 Learning Hint

On the SAT, when you see a long phrase between the subject and verb, **cross it out mentally**. The answer almost always depends on matching the verb to the *real* subject, not the nearest noun.
