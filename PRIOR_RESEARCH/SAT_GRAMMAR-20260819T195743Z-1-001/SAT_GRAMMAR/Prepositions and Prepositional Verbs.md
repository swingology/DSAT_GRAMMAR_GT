# Prepositions and Prepositional Verbs

> Prepositions show up constantly on the SAT — both as **distractors** in [[Subject-Verb Agreement|agreement questions]] and as part of **idiomatic expressions**. Understanding all the ways they function is essential.

---

## Prepositional Phrases

A prepositional phrase consists of a **preposition + its object** (a noun or pronoun). They act as either adjectives or adverbs.

> *As an adjective:* The painting **on the wall** is beautiful. *(Which painting? The one on the wall.)*
> *As an adverb:* She studied **in the library**. *(Where did she study? In the library.)*

### Why This Matters on the SAT

Prepositional phrases are the **#1 way the SAT separates a subject from its verb** to trick you. Train yourself to mentally bracket them off:

> The results [of the experiment] [conducted over several months] **were** inconclusive.
> *(Cross out both prepositional phrases → "The results were inconclusive.")*

See [[Subject-Verb Agreement]] for more examples of this pattern.

---

## Prepositional Verbs (Phrasal Verbs)

Prepositional verbs are verbs that combine with a preposition to create a **meaning different from the verb alone**. The preposition is essential — removing or changing it changes the meaning entirely.

| Prepositional Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| look **into** | investigate | *The committee will look **into** the allegations.* |
| look **after** | take care of | *She looks **after** her younger siblings.* |
| look **up** | search for | *I need to look **up** that definition.* |
| look **over** | review | *Please look **over** this draft.* |
| account **for** | explain | *How do you account **for** the discrepancy?* |
| rely **on** | depend on | *Students rely **on** their notes during review.* |
| consist **of** | be made up of | *The exam consists **of** four sections.* |
| result **in** | cause | *Poor planning resulted **in** delays.* |
| refer **to** | mention / direct to | *The author refers **to** several studies.* |

## SAT Idiomatic Preposition Pairings

The SAT frequently tests whether you've chosen the **correct preposition** with a given word. These are not "rules" you can derive logically — you need to memorize them.

| Correct Pairing | Example | Common Wrong Version |
|---|---|---|
| different **from** | *Her approach is different **from** mine.* | ~~different than~~ |
| distinguish **from** | *It's hard to distinguish fact **from** fiction.* | ~~distinguish between~~ |
| contrast **with** | *The author contrasts city life **with** rural life.* | ~~contrast to~~ |
| associate **with** | *Most people associate rain **with** gloom.* | ~~associate to~~ |
| attribute **to** | *She attributed her success **to** hard work.* | ~~attribute with~~ |
| comply **with** | *All students must comply **with** the policy.* | ~~comply to~~ |
| contribute **to** | *Exercise contributes **to** good health.* | ~~contribute for~~ |
| prefer X **to** Y | *I prefer coffee **to** tea.* | ~~prefer over~~ |
| prohibit **from** | *The rules prohibit students **from** leaving.* | ~~prohibit against~~ |
| sympathize **with** | *I sympathize **with** your frustration.* | ~~sympathize for~~ |

---

## 💡 Learning Hint

When an SAT question underlines a preposition, **read the full verb + preposition combination out loud**. Your ear is often better at catching wrong pairings than your analytical brain. If two answers only differ by preposition, it's testing idiomatic usage — trust the pairing that sounds natural in formal writing.
