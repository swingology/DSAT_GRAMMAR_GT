# Adverbs

> An **adverb** modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. The SAT tests whether you've chosen the right modifier type (adverb vs. adjective), placed it correctly, and used the right form for comparisons.

---

## What Adverbs Do

Adverbs answer: **How? When? Where? To what extent? How often?**

| Question Answered | Adverb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| How? | carefully | She **carefully** examined the data. |
| When? | recently | The study was **recently** published. |
| Where? | everywhere | The news spread **everywhere**. |
| To what extent? | extremely | The results were **extremely** significant. |
| How often? | frequently | He **frequently** cited primary sources. |

---

## Forming Adverbs

Most adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to an adjective — but not all.

| Adjective | Adverb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| quick | quick**ly** | She finished **quickly**. |
| careful | careful**ly** | He read **carefully**. |
| beautiful | beautiful**ly** | The sonata was played **beautifully**. |
| easy | easi**ly** | They solved it **easily**. |
| happy | happi**ly** | She **happily** accepted. |

### Irregular Adverbs (No -ly)

Some adverbs don't follow the -ly pattern. These are SAT favorites because they look like adjectives.

| Adjective | Adverb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| good | **well** | She sings **well**. *(NOT "good")* |
| fast | **fast** | He runs **fast**. *(NOT "fastly")* |
| hard | **hard** | She works **hard**. *(NOT "hardly" — that means "barely")* |
| late | **late** | He arrived **late**. *(NOT "lately" — that means "recently")* |
| straight | **straight** | Walk **straight** ahead. *(NOT "straightly")* |
| early | **early** | She woke up **early**. |

### ⚠️ Dangerous -ly Pairs

Some -ly words exist but mean something **completely different** from the base adjective:

| Word | Meaning | -ly Form | Different Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| hard | with effort | **hardly** | barely, almost not |
| late | not on time | **lately** | recently |
| near | close by | **nearly** | almost |
| scarce | rare | **scarcely** | barely |
| short | not long | **shortly** | soon |

> ❌ He studied **hardly** for the exam. *(means he barely studied)*
> ✅ He studied **hard** for the exam. *(means he put in effort)*

---

## SAT Trap #1: Adjective vs. Adverb

The SAT tests whether you know **what** the word is modifying.

**Modifying a verb → use an adverb:**
> ✅ The experiment progressed **smoothly**.
> ❌ The experiment progressed **smooth**.

**Modifying a noun → use an adjective:**
> ✅ The **smooth** progression of the experiment...
> ❌ The **smoothly** progression of the experiment...

**Modifying an adjective → use an adverb:**
> ✅ The results were **remarkably** clear.
> ❌ The results were **remarkable** clear.

**After a [[Linking Verbs vs Action Verbs|linking verb]] → use an adjective (NOT an adverb):**
> ✅ The soup tastes **delicious**. *(describes the soup)*
> ❌ The soup tastes **deliciously**. *(linking verb → adjective)*

### 💡 Learning Hint — The "What Am I Modifying?" Test

1. Find the word in question (the underlined answer choice)
2. Ask: *What does this word describe?*
3. If it describes a **noun** → adjective. If it describes a **verb, adjective, or other adverb** → adverb.

---

## SAT Trap #2: Good vs. Well

This is one of the most commonly tested pairs.

| Word | Part of Speech | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **good** | adjective | Modifies nouns; used after linking verbs |
| **well** | adverb (usually) | Modifies verbs |
| **well** | adjective (health only) | "I feel well" = I feel healthy |

> ✅ She did **well** on the test. *(adverb modifying "did")*
> ❌ She did **good** on the test.

> ✅ The essay was **good**. *(adjective after linking verb "was")*

> ✅ I don't feel **well**. *(adjective meaning "healthy" — special case)*
> ✅ I feel **good** about the decision. *(adjective after linking verb "feel")*

---

## SAT Trap #3: Comparative and Superlative Forms

| Type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Comparative** (2 things) | Add -er OR use "more" | faster, more carefully |
| **Superlative** (3+ things) | Add -est OR use "most" | fastest, most carefully |

### The Rules

**Short adverbs (1 syllable):** add -er / -est
> fast → fast**er** → fast**est**
> hard → hard**er** → hard**est**

**Long adverbs (2+ syllables, especially -ly):** use more / most
> carefully → **more** carefully → **most** carefully
> efficiently → **more** efficiently → **most** efficiently

### SAT Traps with Comparatives

**Never double up:**
> ❌ She runs **more faster** than her sister.
> ✅ She runs **faster** than her sister.

> ❌ This is the **most easiest** solution.
> ✅ This is the **easiest** solution.

**Use comparative for 2, superlative for 3+:**
> ❌ Of the two candidates, she is the **best** qualified. *(only 2 → comparative)*
> ✅ Of the two candidates, she is the **better** qualified.

> ✅ Of all the candidates, she is the **best** qualified. *(3+ → superlative)*

---

## SAT Trap #4: Adverb Placement

Adverbs should be placed **as close as possible** to the word they modify. Misplacement can change meaning or create ambiguity.

> **"Only" placement changes meaning entirely:**
> **Only** she **said** she loved him. *(No one else said it.)*
> She **only said** she loved him. *(She said it but didn't mean it.)*
> She said she loved **only him**. *(She loved him and no one else.)*

> **Misplaced adverb:**
> ❌ The researchers **almost** tested **every** sample. *(They almost tested but didn't?)*
> ✅ The researchers tested **almost every** sample. *(They tested most of them.)*

This connects directly to modifier placement — see [[Participles]] for dangling and misplaced modifier patterns.

## Practice Questions

### Question 1

> The new policy was implemented ______, with minimal disruption to daily operations.

A) smooth
B) smoothly
C) more smoother
D) smoothest

**Answer: B** — "smoothly" is an adverb modifying "implemented" (a verb).

### Question 2

> Of the two proposed solutions, the committee found the second one to work ______.

A) most efficiently
B) more efficient
C) more efficiently
D) efficientest

**Answer: C** — Comparing two things requires the comparative ("more"), and "efficiently" is an adverb modifying "work."

### Question 3

> The ambassador spoke so ______ that the translators struggled to keep pace.

A) rapid
B) rapidly
C) more rapid
D) rapider

**Answer: B** — "rapidly" is an adverb modifying "spoke" (a verb).

# Adverbs

> An **adverb** modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. The SAT tests whether you've chosen the right form (adjective vs. adverb), the right degree (comparative vs. superlative), and the right placement.

See [[00_Essential Grammar Terms]] for the basic definition and [[Linking Verbs vs Action Verbs]] for why verb type determines adjective vs. adverb.

---

## Adjective vs. Adverb: The Core Distinction

**Adjectives** modify nouns. **Adverbs** modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.

Most adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to an adjective:

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| quick | quick**ly** |
| careful | careful**ly** |
| beautiful | beautiful**ly** |
| quiet | quiet**ly** |
| complete | complete**ly** |

### The SAT Test

Ask yourself: **"What is this word modifying?"**

- If it modifies a **noun** → adjective
- If it modifies a **verb, adjective, or adverb** → adverb

> ✅ She spoke **softly**. *(modifies the verb "spoke" → adverb)*
> ❌ She spoke **soft**. *(adjective can't modify a verb)*

> ✅ The **incredibly** fast runner won. *(modifies the adjective "fast" → adverb)*
> ❌ The **incredible** fast runner won.

**💡 Learning Hint:** After an action verb, you almost always need an adverb. After a [[Linking Verbs vs Action Verbs|linking verb]], you need an adjective. This is the single most important rule for adverb questions.

---

## Good vs. Well

This is one of the SAT's favorite traps.

- **Good** = adjective (modifies nouns)
- **Well** = adverb (modifies verbs) — *except* when describing health

> ✅ She did **well** on the test. *(adverb modifying "did")*
> ❌ She did **good** on the test.

> ✅ The food tastes **good**. *(adjective after linking verb "tastes")*
> ❌ The food tastes **well**.

> ✅ I don't feel **well**. *(exception — "well" as adjective meaning "healthy")*
> ✅ I feel **good** about my decision. *(adjective describing emotional state)*

### The Decision

| Context | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| After an action verb | **well** | Adverb modifies verb |
| After a linking verb (describing quality) | **good** | Adjective modifies subject |
| Describing health | **well** | Special adjective meaning "healthy" |

**💡 Learning Hint:** "Superman does **good** (heroic deeds — noun). You do **well** (adverb — how you perform)."

---

## Bad vs. Badly

Same logic as good vs. well:

> ✅ I feel **bad** about what happened. *(adjective after linking verb — describes "I")*
> ❌ I feel **badly** about what happened. *("feel badly" means your sense of touch is impaired)*

> ✅ She played **badly**. *(adverb modifying the action verb "played")*
> ❌ She played **bad**.

---

## Hard vs. Hardly (and Other Tricky Pairs)

Some adverbs don't end in -ly, and adding -ly changes the meaning entirely.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **hard** (adverb) | with great effort | *She studied **hard**.* |
| **hardly** (adverb) | barely, scarcely | *She **hardly** studied.* |
| **late** (adverb) | not on time | *He arrived **late**.* |
| **lately** (adverb) | recently | *I haven't seen him **lately**.* |
| **near** (adverb) | close by | *The school is **near**.* |
| **nearly** (adverb) | almost | *She **nearly** missed the bus.* |
| **high** (adverb) | at a great height | *The bird flew **high**.* |
| **highly** (adverb) | to a great degree | *She is **highly** skilled.* |

**SAT Trap:** The test will offer "hardly" when the sentence means "with effort," or "lately" when the sentence means "not on time." Read carefully.

---

## Real vs. Really

- **Real** = adjective (modifies nouns)
- **Really** = adverb (modifies verbs, adjectives, adverbs)

> ✅ She is **really** talented. *(adverb modifying "talented")*
> ❌ She is **real** talented. *(informal — wrong on the SAT)*

---

## Comparative and Superlative Forms

| Category | Comparative (2 things) | Superlative (3+ things) |
|---|---|---|
| Short adverbs (1 syllable) | fast**er**, hard**er** | fast**est**, hard**est** |
| Long adverbs (2+ syllables) | **more** quickly | **most** quickly |
| Irregular | better, worse, farther, less | best, worst, farthest, least |

### SAT Rules

**Rule 1: Never combine "more/most" with "-er/-est"**
> ❌ She runs **more faster** than her sister.
> ✅ She runs **faster** than her sister.

> ❌ That was the **most easiest** question.
> ✅ That was the **easiest** question.

**Rule 2: Use comparative for 2 things, superlative for 3+**
> ❌ Of the two candidates, she is the **best** speaker.
> ✅ Of the two candidates, she is the **better** speaker.

> ✅ Of all the candidates, she is the **best** speaker. *(3+)*

**Rule 3: Use "fewer" for countable, "less" for uncountable**
> ✅ **Fewer** students attended. *(countable)*
> ✅ **Less** water was used. *(uncountable)*
> ❌ **Less** students attended.

---

## Adverb Placement

Where you place an adverb can change the meaning of the sentence. The SAT tests this with words like **only, almost, nearly, even, just, merely**.

### "Only" — The Classic Placement Trap

Each placement changes what's being limited:

> **Only** I ate pizza for lunch. *(Nobody else ate it.)*
> I **only** ate pizza for lunch. *(I didn't do anything else with it — just ate it.)*
> I ate **only** pizza for lunch. *(I ate nothing else.)*
> I ate pizza **only** for lunch. *(Not for dinner — just lunch.)*

### The SAT Rule

Place the adverb **as close as possible** to the word it modifies.

> ❌ She **almost** found **every** error in the report.
> *(This says she "almost found" them — like she nearly found them but didn't.)*
> ✅ She found **almost every** error in the report.
> *(She found most of them.)*

> ❌ The medication **nearly** cured **all** of the patients.
> *(It "nearly cured" — close but not quite?)*
> ✅ The medication cured **nearly all** of the patients.
> *(Most were cured.)*

**💡 Learning Hint:** When you see "only," "almost," "nearly," "even," "just," or "merely" underlined on the SAT, test each answer choice by asking: *What exactly is this word limiting?* Move it around mentally to see which placement matches the intended meaning.

## Practice Questions

### Question 1

> The new policy was implemented **quick / quickly**, surprising many employees.

**Correct: quickly** — "implemented" is an action verb, so it needs an adverb.

### Question 2

> Of the two proposals, the board found the second to be **more effective / most effective**.

**Correct: more effective** — comparing two things requires the comparative form.

### Question 3

> The scientist's results, though preliminary, look **promisingly / promising** for future research.

**Correct: promising** — "look" is a [[Linking Verbs vs Action Verbs|linking verb]] here (substitute "are" — "the results are promising" works), so use an adjective.

### Question 4

> The orchestra performed the symphony so **bad / badly** that several audience members left at intermission.

**Correct: badly** — "performed" is an action verb, so it needs an adverb.
