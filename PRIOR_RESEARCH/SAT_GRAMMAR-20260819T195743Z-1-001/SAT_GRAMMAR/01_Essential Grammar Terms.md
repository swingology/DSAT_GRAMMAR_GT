# Essential Grammar Terms

> These are building blocks you need to recognize in order to understand SAT grammar questions. Master these definitions before diving into specific rules.

---

## Core Parts of Speech

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Noun** | A person, place, thing, or idea | *dog, freedom, Chicago* |
| **Verb** | An action or state of being | *run, is, seems* |
| **Adjective** | Modifies a noun | *the **tall** building* |
| **Adverb** | Modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb | *She ran **quickly**.* |
| **Preposition** | Shows relationship between nouns | *in, on, at, by, with, for, to, from* |
| **Conjunction** | Connects words, phrases, or clauses | *and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet* |

See [[Prepositions and Prepositional Verbs]] for an in-depth look at how prepositions function and how the SAT tests them.

---

## Sentence Components

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | The noun performing the action | ***The cat*** *sat on the mat.* |
| **Predicate** | The part of the sentence containing the verb and describing the subject | *The cat **sat on the mat**.* |
| **Object** | The noun receiving the action | *She kicked **the ball**.* |
| **Prepositional Phrase** | A preposition + its object; often separates subject from verb | *of the students, in the room* |
| **Modifier** | A word or phrase that describes another element | *The **brightly colored** bird sang.* |
| **Appositive** | A noun phrase that renames or explains another noun, set off by commas | *My brother, **a talented chef**, cooked dinner.* |

Understanding the **subject** is essential for [[Subject-Verb Agreement]]. Understanding **objects** helps with [[Pronouns|pronoun case]].

---

## Clauses

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Clause** | A group of words with a subject and verb | *Because **she was tired*** |
| **Independent Clause** | A clause that can stand alone as a sentence | ***She was tired.*** |
| **Dependent Clause** | Cannot stand alone; begins with a subordinating conjunction or relative pronoun | ***Because she was tired**, she left early.* |
| **Antecedent** | The noun a [[Pronouns|pronoun]] refers back to | ***Maria** said **she** was coming.* |

---

## Predicate Types

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Predicate Nominative** | A noun that follows a linking verb and renames the subject | *She is **a doctor**.* |
| **Predicate Adjective** | An adjective that follows a linking verb and describes the subject | *The soup tastes **delicious**.* |

These are directly tied to [[Linking Verbs vs Action Verbs]] — linking verbs take predicate nominatives and predicate adjectives, **not** objects.
