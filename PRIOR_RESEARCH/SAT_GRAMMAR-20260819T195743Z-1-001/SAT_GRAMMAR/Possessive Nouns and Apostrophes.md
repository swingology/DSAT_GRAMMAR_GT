# Possessive Nouns and Apostrophes

> The SAT loves testing possessive nouns because they look simple but have a "trap" logic based on whether a word is **naturally plural** or was **made plural by adding -s**.

---

## The Two-Question Decision Framework

When you see an apostrophe question on the SAT, ask yourself these two questions **in order**:

**1. Is it possessive?** Does the noun actually *own* the thing that follows it? If not, **no apostrophe is needed at all**.

**2. Is the noun plural?**
- If it ends in **-s** (like *dogs*) → just add the apostrophe: **dogs'**
- If it does **not** end in -s (like *men*) → add **'s**: **men's**

```
                    Does the noun OWN something?
                        /              \
                      YES               NO
                       |                 |
               Is the noun plural?    No apostrophe.
                  /          \        Just write the
                YES          NO       plain noun.
                 |            |
          Does it end      Add 's
          in -s?           (dog's)
           /      \
         YES      NO
          |        |
       Add '     Add 's
      (dogs')   (children's)
```

---

## Irregular Plurals — The "Children's" Rule

Some nouns change their spelling to become plural rather than adding -s. These are **irregular plurals**. Because they don't end in -s, you add **'s** to show possession — just like you would with a singular noun.

> ✅ The **children's** toys *(the toys belonging to the children)*
> ❌ The **childrens'** toys *("childrens" is not a word — never correct)*

### Irregular Plurals Reference Table

| Singular | Plural | Possessive Form | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| man | men | **men's** | *the men's locker room* |
| woman | women | **women's** | *women's rights* |
| child | children | **children's** | *the children's playground* |
| mouse | mice | **mice's** | *the mice's cheese* |
| person | people | **people's** | *the people's choice* |
| goose | geese | **geese's** | *the geese's feathers* |
| tooth | teeth | **teeth's** | *the teeth's enamel* |
| foot | feet | **feet's** | *the feet's arches* |
| ox | oxen | **oxen's** | *the oxen's yoke* |

**💡 Learning Hint:** If the plural form looks "weird" (no -s at the end), treat it like a singular noun for apostrophe purposes → add **'s**.

---

## Regular Plurals — The Standard Rule

Most English nouns become plural by adding **-s**. For these, you **only** add an apostrophe after the -s.

| Form | How to Write It | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Singular possessive | noun + **'s** | *the **student's** desk* (one student) |
| Plural possessive | noun + s + **'** | *the **students'** desks* (multiple students) |

More examples:

| Singular Possessive | Plural Possessive |
|---|---|
| the dog**'s** bone | the dog**s'** bones |
| the teacher**'s** desk | the teacher**s'** desks |
| the country**'s** laws | the countrie**s'** laws |
| the boss**'s** office | the bosse**s'** offices |

---

## Singular Nouns Ending in -s

What about singular nouns that already end in -s, like *boss* or *Charles*?

> ✅ The **boss's** office *(one boss owns the office)*
> ✅ **Charles's** book *(one person named Charles)*

The SAT generally accepts adding **'s** to singular nouns ending in -s. The key is recognizing that the noun is **singular**, not plural.

---

## Possessives vs. Plain Plurals — No Apostrophe Needed

The SAT also tests whether you can tell when an apostrophe is **not** needed at all. A noun that is simply plural (not owning anything) gets **no apostrophe**.

> ✅ The **dogs** ran across the yard. *(just plural, no possession)*
> ❌ The **dog's** ran across the yard. *(nothing is being possessed)*

> ✅ Three **countries** signed the treaty. *(plural)*
> ✅ Three **countries'** leaders signed the treaty. *(plural possessive)*

---

## Its vs. It's — A Special Case

This is one of the most commonly tested distinctions on the SAT.

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **its** | possessive (belongs to it) | *The dog wagged **its** tail.* |
| **it's** | contraction of "it is" or "it has" | ***It's** been a long day.* |
| **its'** | ⛔ **NOT A WORD. Never pick this.** | — |

**💡 Learning Hint:** "It's" **always** means "it is." If you can't substitute "it is" into the sentence, use **its** (no apostrophe). And if you see **its'** as an answer choice, eliminate it immediately — it doesn't exist.

The same logic applies to other possessive [[Pronouns]]: **your/you're**, **their/they're**, **whose/who's**. Possessive pronouns **never** take apostrophes.

---

## SAT Traps Summary

| Trap | Why It's Wrong | Correct Form |
|---|---|---|
| **childrens'** | "Childrens" isn't a word | **children's** |
| **womens'** | "Womens" isn't a word | **women's** |
| **geeses'** | "Geeses" isn't a word | **geese's** |
| **students's** | Double possessive doesn't exist | **students'** |
| **it's** (as possessive) | "It's" = "it is" | **its** |
| **its'** | Not a real word — ever | **its** |
| **who's** (as possessive) | "Who's" = "who is" | **whose** |

## Practice Questions

### Question 1 (High Difficulty)

> The geese migratory patterns were disrupted by the citys expansion into the wetlands.

A) geeses ... citys'
B) geese's ... city's
C) geeses' ... citys
D) geese ... city's

**Correct Answer: B**
*Why?* "Geese" is an irregular plural (doesn't end in -s) → needs **'s** → *geese's*. "City" is a singular noun owning the expansion → needs **'s** → *city's*.

### Question 2

> The three actress dressing rooms were renovated, but the director office remained untouched.

A) actress' ... director's
B) actresses' ... director's
C) actress's ... directors
D) actresses ... director's

**Correct Answer: B**
*Why?* Three actresses (regular plural ending in -s) → **actresses'**. One director (singular) → **director's**.

### Question 3

> The women volleyball team celebrated after the championship, crediting the coach leadership and the fan support.

A) women's ... coach's ... fans'
B) womens' ... coach's ... fan's
C) women's ... coachs' ... fans'
D) women's ... coach's ... fan's

**Correct Answer: A**
*Why?* "Women" is an irregular plural → **women's**. "Coach" is singular → **coach's**. "Fans" is a regular plural → **fans'**.
