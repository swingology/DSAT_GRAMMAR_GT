# Comma Rules

> Commas are the most versatile — and most tested — punctuation mark on the SAT. The key insight: every comma must have a **grammatical reason** to be there. If you can't name the rule, the comma probably doesn't belong.

---

## Rule 1: Comma + FANBOYS Between Two Independent Clauses

> ✅ She studied all night**, and** she passed the exam.
> ❌ She studied all night**,** she passed the exam. *(comma splice)*

You need **both** the comma **and** the coordinating conjunction. See [[Sentence Boundaries and Clause Joining]] for full details.

**Note:** If what follows the FANBOYS is NOT an independent clause (no new subject), you usually don't need a comma:

> ✅ She studied all night **and** passed the exam. *(no comma — "passed the exam" has no new subject)*

---

## Rule 2: After an Introductory Element

When a sentence starts with a dependent clause, transitional phrase, or introductory element, follow it with a comma.

> ✅ **After the concert,** we went to dinner.
> ✅ **However,** the results were inconclusive.
> ✅ **Running through the park,** she tripped on a root. *(see [[Participles]])*
> ✅ **In 2024,** the company expanded internationally.

---

## Rule 3: Around Non-Essential (Non-Restrictive) Phrases

If a phrase can be removed without changing the core meaning, it must be enclosed in commas. See [[Colons Semicolons and Dashes]] for the full essential vs. non-essential breakdown.

> ✅ The CEO**,** who founded the company in 2010**,** announced her retirement.
> ✅ My dog**,** a golden retriever**,** loves swimming. *(appositive)*

**The Matching Rule:** If you open with a comma, you must close with a comma. Don't orphan one side.

> ❌ The CEO, who founded the company in 2010 announced her retirement.
> ✅ The CEO, who founded the company in 2010**,** announced her retirement.

---

## Rule 4: Items in a Series (Oxford Comma)

> ✅ She bought apples**,** oranges**,** and bananas.

The SAT generally uses the Oxford comma (comma before "and" in a list). Three or more items in a series should be separated by commas.

---

## Rule 5: Coordinate Adjectives

If two adjectives **independently** modify the same noun, separate them with a comma. If one adjective modifies the other + noun together, no comma.

**Test 1 — The "and" test:** Can you put "and" between them?
**Test 2 — The swap test:** Can you reverse their order?

> ✅ She is a **talented, dedicated** researcher. *("talented and dedicated" works; "dedicated, talented" works)*
> ❌ She wore a **bright, red** jacket. *("bright and red" sounds odd — "bright" modifies "red jacket" as a unit)*
> ✅ She wore a **bright red** jacket.

---

## Where Commas Do NOT Belong

These are common SAT traps — unnecessary commas that students add because they "feel right."

### No Comma Between Subject and Verb

> ❌ The president of the company**,** announced new policies.
> ✅ The president of the company announced new policies.

### No Comma Between Verb and Object

> ❌ She announced**,** that the project was complete.
> ✅ She announced that the project was complete.

### No Comma Before/After a Prepositional Phrase That's Essential

> ❌ The book**,** on the table**,** is mine.
> ✅ The book on the table is mine. *(essential — identifies which book)*

### No Comma in a Compound Subject or Compound Verb (two items)

> ❌ The teacher**,** and the principal met.
> ✅ The teacher and the principal met.

**💡 Learning Hint:** If an answer choice on the SAT adds a comma, ask: "What rule justifies this comma?" If you can't name one from this list, the comma is probably wrong.

## Practice Questions

### Question 1

> Dr. Martinez, a renowned physicist from Stanford conducted groundbreaking research on dark matter.

A) NO CHANGE
B) Dr. Martinez, a renowned physicist from Stanford, conducted
C) Dr. Martinez a renowned physicist from Stanford, conducted
D) Dr. Martinez a renowned physicist from Stanford conducted

**Correct Answer: B**
*Why?* "A renowned physicist from Stanford" is an appositive (non-essential phrase). It needs commas on **both** sides.

### Question 2

> The experimental, treatment showed promise in early clinical trials.

A) NO CHANGE
B) The experimental treatment, showed
C) The experimental treatment showed
D) The, experimental treatment showed

**Correct Answer: C**
*Why?* "Experimental" and "treatment" don't pass the "and" test ("experimental and treatment"?). No comma needed. And there should never be a comma between an adjective and its noun (D) or between subject and verb (B).
