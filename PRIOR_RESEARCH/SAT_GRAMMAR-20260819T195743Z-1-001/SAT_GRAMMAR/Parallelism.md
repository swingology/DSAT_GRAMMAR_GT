# Parallelism

> **Parallel structure** means that items in a list, comparison, or paired construction must be in the **same grammatical form**. The SAT is obsessed with this rule — it appears on nearly every test.

---

## The Core Principle

When you join or list grammatical elements, they must match in form:

- Nouns with nouns
- Verbs with verbs (same tense/form)
- Phrases with phrases of the same type
- Clauses with clauses

> ❌ She likes **hiking**, **swimming**, and **to run**.
> *(Two gerunds + one infinitive — not parallel)*
> ✅ She likes **hiking**, **swimming**, and **running**.
> *(All gerunds — parallel)*

> ❌ The job requires **creativity**, **dedication**, and **being patient**.
> *(Two nouns + one gerund phrase — not parallel)*
> ✅ The job requires **creativity**, **dedication**, and **patience**.
> *(All nouns — parallel)*

---

## Parallelism in Lists

The most common pattern. Every item in a series must share the same grammatical form.

| Not Parallel | Parallel | Why |
|---|---|---|
| to read, writing, and **to speak** | to read, to write, and **to speak** | All infinitives |
| smart, athletic, and **has charm** | smart, athletic, and **charming** | All adjectives |
| in the morning, at noon, and **when evening comes** | in the morning, at noon, and **in the evening** | All prepositional phrases |
| She **planned** the event, **organized** the team, and **was managing** the budget | She **planned** the event, **organized** the team, and **managed** the budget | All past tense verbs |

**💡 Learning Hint:** When you see a list of 3+ items on the SAT, check the form of the first item. Every other item must match it exactly. If item 1 is a gerund (-ing), all items must be gerunds.

---

## Parallelism in Comparisons

When comparing two things, the structures on each side must match.

> ❌ **Running** a marathon is harder than **to swim** a mile.
> ✅ **Running** a marathon is harder than **swimming** a mile.

> ❌ The research of Dr. Smith was more thorough than **Dr. Jones**.
> *(Compares "research" to "Dr. Jones" — not equivalent)*
> ✅ The research of Dr. Smith was more thorough than **that of Dr. Jones**.
> *(Compares "research" to "that [research]" — parallel)*

### Comparison Signal Words

Watch for these — they almost always require parallel structure:

- **than** (more X than Y)
- **as...as** (as X as Y)
- **rather than** / **instead of**
- **like** / **unlike**

---

## Correlative Conjunctions

These come in **fixed pairs**. If you see one half, the other half must appear — and the structures after each half must be parallel.

| Pair | Example (Parallel) |
|---|---|
| **either...or** | She will **either** take the bus **or** drive her car. |
| **neither...nor** | He is **neither** talented **nor** hardworking. |
| **not only...but also** | The plan is **not only** ambitious **but also** practical. |
| **both...and** | She is **both** a scientist **and** an artist. |
| **whether...or** | I don't know **whether** to stay **or** to go. |

### The Parallelism Test for Correlatives

Whatever grammatical form follows the first word must follow the second:

> ❌ She **not only** sings **but also** is a great dancer.
> *(After "not only" = verb. After "but also" = noun phrase. Not parallel.)*
> ✅ She is **not only** a great singer **but also** a great dancer.
> *(Both = noun phrases.)*

> ❌ The city **both** improved its parks **and** the roads were repaired.
> *(After "both" = verb phrase. After "and" = full clause. Not parallel.)*
> ✅ The city **both** improved its parks **and** repaired its roads.
> *(Both = verb phrases with matching structure.)*

**💡 Learning Hint:** For correlative conjunctions, draw a line from one half of the pair to the other. Whatever comes immediately after the first word must have a grammatical twin immediately after the second word.

---

## Parallelism with Verbs

When a subject has multiple verbs, they must all be in the same tense and form.

> ❌ The scientist **collected** data, **analyzed** the results, and **is publishing** her findings.
> *(Two past tense + one present progressive — not parallel)*
> ✅ The scientist **collected** data, **analyzed** the results, and **published** her findings.

This connects to [[Verb Tense and Mood]] — tense consistency within a sentence is a form of parallelism.

---

## SAT Strategy: How to Spot Parallelism Questions

1. **Look for lists** — any time you see commas separating three or more items
2. **Look for conjunctions** — and, but, or, nor, and the correlative pairs
3. **Look for comparisons** — than, as...as, rather than, like/unlike
4. **Check the form** of the first element, then verify all others match
5. **Read the underlined portion** in context of the full list or comparison

## Practice Questions

### Question 1

> The internship program aims to provide students with practical experience, professional mentorship, and **helping them build networks**.

A) NO CHANGE
B) to help them build networks
C) networking opportunities
D) they can build networks

**Correct Answer: C**
*Why?* The list has three items after "provide students with": (1) practical experience, (2) professional mentorship, (3) ___. Items 1 and 2 are noun phrases, so item 3 must also be a noun phrase. Only C ("networking opportunities") matches.

### Question 2

> The new policy not only reduces costs but also **it improves efficiency**.

A) NO CHANGE
B) improving efficiency
C) improves efficiency
D) to improve efficiency

**Correct Answer: C**
*Why?* "Not only **reduces** costs" → "but also **improves** efficiency." The structure after each half of the correlative pair must match. "Reduces" = present tense verb → "improves" = present tense verb.

### Question 3

> Writing a novel is more challenging than **to write** a short story.

A) NO CHANGE
B) when you write
C) writing
D) if one writes

**Correct Answer: C**
*Why?* "**Writing** a novel" is compared to something else using "than." The comparison must be parallel: "**Writing** a novel is more challenging than **writing** a short story."
