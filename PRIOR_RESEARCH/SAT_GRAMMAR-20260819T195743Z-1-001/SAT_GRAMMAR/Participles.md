# Participles

> A **participle** is a word formed from a verb that functions as an **adjective**. It describes a noun or a pronoun. On the SAT, the real challenge is the **participial phrase** — a group of words consisting of a participle plus its modifiers or complements, acting like one giant adjective.

Participles are a special type of [[00_Essential Grammar Terms|modifier]]. Unlike [[Adverbs]], which modify verbs, participles modify **nouns** — they're verb forms acting as adjectives.

---

## The Two Types of Participles

| Type | How It's Formed | What It Describes | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Present participle** | verb + **-ing** | Something **doing** the action | The **crying** baby. / She **is running**. |
| **Past participle** | verb + **-ed, -en, -d, -t, -n** (or irregular) | Something that has **received** the action | The **broken** window. / The vase **was broken**. |

### Past Participle Endings to Recognize

| Ending | Examples |
|---|---|
| **-ed** | walk**ed**, paint**ed**, exhaust**ed** |
| **-en** | brok**en**, chos**en**, writt**en**, fall**en** |
| **-d** | sai**d**, pai**d**, hear**d** |
| **-t** | spen**t**, buil**t**, lef**t**, los**t** |
| **-n** | see**n**, grow**n**, know**n**, blow**n** |
| **Irregular** | run, cut, put, shut *(same as base form)* |

---

## The -ing vs. -ed Trap

The SAT tests whether you understand the difference between the **cause** and the **receiver** of a feeling.

| -ing (Cause) | -ed (Receiver) |
|---|---|
| The movie was **boring**. *(causes boredom)* | The students were **bored**. *(feel boredom)* |
| The news was **surprising**. | She was **surprised**. |
| The lecture was **confusing**. | The audience was **confused**. |
| The results were **encouraging**. | The researchers were **encouraged**. |
| The task was **exhausting**. | The workers were **exhausted**. |
| A **devastating** storm. | A **devastated** community. |
| An **interesting** book. | An **interested** reader. |

> ❌ The students were **boring** by the lecture. *(This says the students cause boredom.)*
> ✅ The students were **bored** by the lecture.

> ❌ The results were **surprised**. *(Results can't feel surprise.)*
> ✅ The results were **surprising**.

**💡 Learning Hint:** Ask: "Is this noun the **cause** or the **receiver** of the feeling?" Cause → -ing. Receiver → -ed.

## Participial Phrases

A participial phrase is a participle plus its modifiers and complements, acting as **one giant adjective** for a specific noun.

### Placement and Punctuation Rules

**Introductory (start of sentence):** Phrase first, comma, then the noun it describes must come **immediately after** the comma.

> ✅ **Frustrated by the delay**, the passenger checked his watch.

**Non-Essential Interrupter (middle of sentence):** Set off by commas on both sides.

> ✅ The pirate ship, **sailing through the fog**, approached the island.
> ✅ The scientist, **known for her groundbreaking research**, received the award.

**End of sentence:** Follows the noun it modifies.

> ✅ The students left the exam **feeling confident about their answers**.

**Essential Phrases (No Commas):** If the phrase is needed to identify which noun you mean.

> ✅ The student **sitting in the front row** raised her hand.
> *(Which student? Essential — no commas.)*

## SAT Trap #1: Dangling Modifiers (The Big One)

> [!WARNING]
> A **dangling modifier** occurs when a participial phrase doesn't logically modify the subject that follows it. This is the **#1 way the SAT tests participles**.

### The Rule

A participial phrase at the **beginning of a sentence** MUST modify the subject immediately after the comma. If the noun following the phrase is NOT the thing doing (or receiving) the action, it dangles.

> ❌ **Walking through the museum**, the paintings were breathtaking.
> *(Who is walking? "The paintings" — but paintings can't walk.)*
> ✅ **Walking through the museum**, **we** found the paintings breathtaking.

> ❌ **Having studied all night**, the exam seemed easy.
> *(The exam didn't study all night.)*
> ✅ **Having studied all night**, **she** found the exam easy.

> ❌ **Revised multiple times**, the editor finally approved the article.
> *(The editor wasn't revised.)*
> ✅ **Revised multiple times**, **the article** was finally approved by the editor.

**💡 Learning Hint — The "Who Is Doing This?" Test:**
1. Read the opening phrase
2. Ask: **Who or what is performing the action?**
3. Check: **Is that who appears right after the comma as the subject?**
4. If not → dangling modifier → wrong answer

## SAT Trap #2: Misplaced Modifiers

Unlike danglers (where the correct subject is missing), **misplaced modifiers** have the right subject somewhere — it's just in the wrong position.

> ❌ The researcher presented findings to the board **wearing a lab coat**.
> *(The board is wearing a lab coat?)*
> ✅ **Wearing a lab coat**, the researcher presented findings to the board.

See [[Adverbs]] for more on adverb placement and the "only/almost/nearly" trap.

---

## SAT Trap #3: Participle vs. Main Verb — The Fragment Trap

A participle **alone cannot be the main verb** of a sentence. It needs a **helping verb** (is, was, has, were, has been, etc.) to function as the predicate. Without one, you have a fragment.

> ❌ The student **studying** for the SAT for three hours.
> *(Fragment — no main verb.)*
> ✅ The student **was studying** for the SAT for three hours.
> ✅ The student **studying** for the SAT **fell asleep**. *(participle = adjective; "fell" = main verb)*

| Structure | Role | Complete? |
|---|---|---|
| The dog **running** across the yard. | Participle as adjective — but no main verb | ❌ Fragment |
| The dog **was running** across the yard. | Helping verb + participle = main verb | ✅ Complete |
| The dog **running** across the yard **barked** loudly. | "Running" = adjective, "barked" = main verb | ✅ Complete |

**💡 Learning Hint:** If the only "verb-like" words in a clause are -ing forms without a helper, there's no real verb → it's a fragment.

---

## SAT Trap #4: Perfect Participles for Sequence

Use **having + past participle** to show that one action was completed **before** another.

> ❌ **Finishing** the exam, she left the room.
> *(Ambiguous — simultaneous?)*
> ✅ **Having finished** the exam, she left the room.
> *(Clear — finishing happened first.)*

| Form | Timing | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present participle (-ing) | Simultaneous | **Smiling broadly**, she accepted the award. |
| Perfect participle (having + past participle) | Completed before | **Having completed** the course, he applied. |
| Past participle (-ed) | Passive/state | **Exhausted** from the hike, they rested. |

## Common Irregular Past Participles

The SAT expects you to know the correct past participle form for common irregular verbs.

| Base | Simple Past | Past Participle |
|---|---|---|
| begin | began | **begun** |
| break | broke | **broken** |
| choose | chose | **chosen** |
| do | did | **done** |
| drink | drank | **drunk** |
| drive | drove | **driven** |
| eat | ate | **eaten** |
| forget | forgot | **forgotten** |
| give | gave | **given** |
| go | went | **gone** |
| grow | grew | **grown** |
| hide | hid | **hidden** |
| know | knew | **known** |
| lie (recline) | lay | **lain** |
| lay (place) | laid | **laid** |
| ride | rode | **ridden** |
| ring | rang | **rung** |
| rise | rose | **risen** |
| run | ran | **run** |
| see | saw | **seen** |
| shake | shook | **shaken** |
| shrink | shrank | **shrunk** |
| sing | sang | **sung** |
| speak | spoke | **spoken** |
| steal | stole | **stolen** |
| swim | swam | **swum** |
| take | took | **taken** |
| throw | threw | **thrown** |
| wake | woke | **woken** |
| wear | wore | **worn** |
| write | wrote | **written** |

> ❌ She had **went** to the store. → ✅ She had **gone** to the store.
> ❌ The bell had **rang**. → ✅ The bell had **rung**.
> ❌ He has **drove** across the country. → ✅ He has **driven** across the country.

**💡 Learning Hint:** If the verb follows "has," "have," or "had," you need the **past participle** (third column), not the simple past (second column).

## Practice Questions

### Question 1 — Past Participle + Dangling Modifier

> ______ by decades of research, the theory gained widespread acceptance in the scientific community.

A) Supporting
B) Supported
C) Having supporting
D) Being supported

**Answer: B** — The theory is *receiving* support (passive), so the past participle "Supported" is correct.

### Question 2 — Perfect Participle for Sequence

> ______ the initial data, the researchers decided to expand their sample size before drawing conclusions.

A) Reviewing
B) Having reviewed
C) Reviewed
D) Being reviewed

**Answer: B** — The reviewing happened *before* the decision. "Having reviewed" correctly shows this sequence.

### Question 3 — Adjective vs. Participle

> The ambassador, ______ in three languages, served as the primary negotiator during the summit.

A) fluently
B) being fluent
C) fluent
D) having fluently

**Answer: C** — "Fluent" is an adjective in an appositive phrase modifying "ambassador." The [[Adverbs|adverb]] "fluently" can't modify a noun.

### Question 4 — Dangling Modifier Fix

> ______, the funding proposal outlined a five-year plan for infrastructure development.

A) Writing by the committee carefully
B) The committee carefully writing it
C) Carefully written by the committee
D) The committee having written it carefully

**Answer: C** — The participial phrase must modify "the funding proposal." The proposal was *written* (passive), so "Carefully written by the committee" is correct.

### Question 5 — High Difficulty Dangling Modifier (Morpho Butterfly)

> Found primarily in the tropical rainforests of Central and South America, the vivid blue wings of the Morpho butterfly are actually a result of light diffraction rather than pigment.

A) NO CHANGE
B) the Morpho butterfly has vivid blue wings that are actually a result of light diffraction
C) light diffraction results in the vivid blue wings of the Morpho butterfly
D) the Morpho butterfly's vivid blue wings are actually a result of light diffraction

**Answer: B** — "Found in rainforests" describes the **butterfly**, not its wings or light diffraction. Only B places "the Morpho butterfly" as the subject. D is tricky — the possessive makes "wings" the subject, not the butterfly.

### Question 6 — -ing vs. -ed

> The documentary about climate change was deeply **disturbed / disturbing**, prompting many viewers to take action.

**Answer: disturbing** — The documentary is the *cause* of the feeling → -ing form.

### Question 7 — High Difficulty Dangling Modifier (Dr. Aris)

> Having spent decades studying the migratory patterns of Arctic terns, the impact of climate change on the birds' flight paths was finally clear to Dr. Aris.

A) NO CHANGE
B) the birds' flight paths were impacted by climate change, which Dr. Aris saw.
C) Dr. Aris finally understood how climate change impacted the birds' flight paths.
D) climate change's impact on the birds' flight paths was clear to Dr. Aris.

**Answer: C** — The modifier "Having spent decades studying..." describes a **person** (someone who can study). Only C places **Dr. Aris** as the subject immediately after the comma. In A, the subject is "impact." In B, it's "flight paths." In D, it's "climate change's impact." None of those can spend decades studying.

## Summary: The 3-Step Participle Check

```
Step 1: IDENTIFY — Is it a participial phrase?
        Look for -ing, -ed, -en, -t, -n at the start of a phrase.
                           ↓
Step 2: MATCH — Does it modify the right noun?
        The noun being described must be the subject
        immediately after the comma (introductory) or
        adjacent to the phrase (mid/end sentence).
        If not → dangling or misplaced modifier.
                           ↓
Step 3: FORM — Is the participle form correct?
        - Cause = -ing, Receiver = -ed
        - Sequence = "having + past participle"
        - Check irregular past participles
        - Make sure there IS a main verb (not a fragment)
```
