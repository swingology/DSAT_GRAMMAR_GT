# Pronouns

> A **pronoun** is a word that replaces a noun (its **antecedent**). The SAT tests whether pronouns are used clearly, correctly, and consistently.

See [[00_Essential Grammar Terms]] for the definition of antecedent and related terms.

---

## Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement

A pronoun must agree in **number and person** with the noun it replaces.

> ❌ Every student must bring **their** laptop.
> ✅ Every student must bring **his or her** laptop.
> ✅ All students must bring **their** laptops.

*("Every student" is singular, so the pronoun must be singular — or restructure the sentence.)*

> ❌ The committee submitted **their** report late.
> ✅ The committee submitted **its** report late.

*(Collective nouns like "committee," "team," "group" are singular when acting as one unit.)*

### 💡 Learning Hint

The SAT loves pairing singular indefinite pronouns (everyone, each, anyone) with "they/their." It's **almost always wrong** in SAT grammar — look for the singular alternative. See [[Subject-Verb Agreement]] for the full list of singular indefinite pronouns.

---

## Ambiguous Pronoun Reference

Every pronoun must have a **clear, unmistakable antecedent**. If two nouns could be the antecedent, the reference is ambiguous.

> ❌ When Sarah met Emily, **she** was nervous.
> *(Who was nervous — Sarah or Emily?)*
> ✅ When Sarah met Emily, **Sarah** was nervous.

> ❌ The report contradicted the study, and **it** was later retracted.
> *(What was retracted — the report or the study?)*
> ✅ The report contradicted the study, and **the report** was later retracted.

### 💡 Learning Hint

When you see "it," "they," "this," or "which" on the SAT, immediately ask: *What specific noun does this refer to?* If you can't point to **exactly one noun**, the answer is probably wrong.

## Pronoun Case (Subject vs. Object)

| Case | Pronouns | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** (nominative) | I, he, she, we, they, who | Does the action |
| **Object** (objective) | me, him, her, us, them, whom | Receives the action |
| **Possessive** | my, his, her, our, their, whose | Shows ownership |

### Common SAT Traps

> ❌ Me and Jake went to the store.
> ✅ **Jake and I** went to the store. *(subject)*

> ❌ The teacher called Jake and I.
> ✅ The teacher called **Jake and me**. *(object)*

> ❌ The student **who** the award was given to...
> ✅ The student **whom** the award was given to...
> *(Rephrase: "The award was given to **him**" → objective case → whom)*

### 💡 Learning Hint — The Remove-the-Other-Person Trick

Not sure whether to use "I" or "me"? **Remove the other person** from the sentence.
- "Me went to the store" sounds wrong → use **I**
- "The teacher called I" sounds wrong → use **me**

---

## Who vs. Whom

- **Who** = subject (he/she/they)
- **Whom** = object (him/her/them)

> **Who** wrote this essay? *(He wrote this essay.)*
> To **whom** was the letter addressed? *(The letter was addressed to him.)*

### 💡 Learning Hint

Substitute "he" or "him." If "him" fits → **whom**. If "he" fits → **who**. (Both "him" and "whom" end in **m**.)
