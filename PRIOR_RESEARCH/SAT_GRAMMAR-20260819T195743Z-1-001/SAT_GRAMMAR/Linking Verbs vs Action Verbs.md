# Linking Verbs vs Action Verbs

> This distinction matters because **linking verbs** take [[00_Essential Grammar Terms|predicate nominatives]] and [[00_Essential Grammar Terms|predicate adjectives]], not objects. The SAT tests whether you use an **adjective** or an **adverb** after a verb.

---

## What Are Linking Verbs?

Linking verbs connect the subject to a word that **describes or renames** it. They do not show action.

**Common linking verbs:** is, am, are, was, were, be, been, being, seem, become, appear, feel, look, sound, taste, smell, remain, stay, grow, turn, prove

---

## The SAT Trap: Adjective vs. Adverb After a Verb

> ✅ The flowers smell **sweet**. *(predicate adjective — "sweet" describes "flowers")*
> ❌ The flowers smell **sweetly**. *(wrong — "smell" is a linking verb here, not an action)*

---

## 💡 Learning Hint — The Substitution Test

If you can replace the verb with **"is/are"** and the sentence still makes sense, it's a linking verb → use an **adjective**, not an adverb.

- "The flowers **are** sweet" ✅ → linking verb → adjective is correct
- "She sings **beautifully**" → "She **is** beautifully" ❌ → action verb → adverb is correct

---

## Dual-Purpose Verbs

Some verbs can be **either** linking or action depending on context:

| Verb | As Linking (use adjective) | As Action (use adverb) |
|---|---|---|
| **feel** | *I feel **bad** about it.* | *She felt **carefully** along the wall.* |
| **look** | *He looks **tired**.* | *She looked **eagerly** through the window.* |
| **smell** | *The soup smells **good**.* | *The dog smelled **excitedly** at the treat.* |
| **taste** | *The cake tastes **delicious**.* | *She tasted the sauce **cautiously**.* |
| **grow** | *The crowd grew **restless**.* | *The garden grew **quickly** this summer.* |
