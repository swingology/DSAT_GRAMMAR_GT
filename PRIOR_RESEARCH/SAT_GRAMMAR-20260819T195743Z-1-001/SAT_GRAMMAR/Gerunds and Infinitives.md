# Gerunds and Infinitives

> A **gerund** is a verb's -ing form used as a **noun**. An **infinitive** is "to + verb" used as a noun, adjective, or adverb. The SAT tests whether you've chosen the right form after certain verbs and in parallel structures.

This is closely related to [[Participles]] (which are -ing/-ed forms used as **adjectives**) and [[Parallelism]] (which requires consistent verb forms in lists).

---

## Gerund vs. Participle vs. Progressive — Same Form, Different Job

The -ing form of a verb can play three completely different roles. Context tells you which:

| Role | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Gerund** | Acts as a **noun** | ***Swimming** is good exercise.* (subject) |
| **Present participle** | Acts as an **adjective** | *The **swimming** duck crossed the pond.* (modifies duck) |
| **Progressive verb** | Acts as the **main verb** (with helper) | *She **is swimming** right now.* (verb phrase) |

See [[Participles]] for the adjective use and the fragment trap.

---

## Verbs Followed by Gerunds Only

Some verbs require the -ing form (gerund) as their object. Using an infinitive after these is wrong.

| Verb | Correct | Incorrect |
|---|---|---|
| enjoy | *She enjoys **reading**.* | ~~She enjoys **to read**.~~ |
| avoid | *He avoids **making** mistakes.* | ~~He avoids **to make** mistakes.~~ |
| consider | *They considered **moving**.* | ~~They considered **to move**.~~ |
| finish | *I finished **writing** the essay.* | ~~I finished **to write** the essay.~~ |
| suggest | *She suggested **going** early.* | ~~She suggested **to go** early.~~ |
| practice | *He practices **playing** daily.* | ~~He practices **to play** daily.~~ |
| deny | *She denied **stealing** the book.* | ~~She denied **to steal** the book.~~ |
| risk | *Don't risk **losing** your spot.* | ~~Don't risk **to lose** your spot.~~ |
| keep | *Keep **working** hard.* | ~~Keep **to work** hard.~~ |
| mind | *Would you mind **helping**?* | ~~Would you mind **to help**?~~ |

**💡 Learning Hint:** Many "gerund-only" verbs involve **ongoing or repeated actions** (enjoy, practice, keep) or **mental processing** (consider, suggest, deny).

---

## Verbs Followed by Infinitives Only

Some verbs require the infinitive form. Using a gerund after these is wrong.

| Verb | Correct | Incorrect |
|---|---|---|
| want | *She wants **to leave**.* | ~~She wants **leaving**.~~ |
| need | *He needs **to study**.* | ~~He needs **studying**.~~ |
| decide | *They decided **to stay**.* | ~~They decided **staying**.~~ |
| plan | *We plan **to travel**.* | ~~We plan **traveling**.~~ |
| hope | *I hope **to see** you.* | ~~I hope **seeing** you.~~ |
| promise | *She promised **to help**.* | ~~She promised **helping**.~~ |
| refuse | *He refused **to answer**.* | ~~He refused **answering**.~~ |
| agree | *They agreed **to cooperate**.* | ~~They agreed **cooperating**.~~ |
| seem | *It seems **to work**.* | ~~It seems **working**.~~ |
| expect | *We expect **to finish**.* | ~~We expect **finishing**.~~ |

**💡 Learning Hint:** Many "infinitive-only" verbs involve **future intent** (plan, hope, promise, decide) or **willingness** (agree, refuse, want).

---

## Verbs That Accept Either (Sometimes with Different Meaning)

Some verbs can take both forms — but the meaning may change:

| Verb | With Gerund | With Infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| **stop** | *She stopped **smoking**.* (quit the habit) | *She stopped **to smoke**.* (paused in order to smoke) |
| **remember** | *I remember **locking** the door.* (I recall doing it) | *I remember **to lock** the door.* (I don't forget to do it) |
| **forget** | *I forgot **meeting** him.* (I don't recall the event) | *I forgot **to meet** him.* (I failed to do it) |
| **try** | *Try **pressing** the button.* (experiment with it) | *Try **to press** the button.* (make an effort) |
| **begin/start** | *She began **singing**.* | *She began **to sing**.* *(Both correct, no meaning change)* |
| **continue** | *He continued **working**.* | *He continued **to work**.* *(Both correct, no meaning change)* |

## The SAT Trap: Mixing Forms

The most common SAT error is creating a hybrid that doesn't match either pattern:

> ❌ The team began **to contributing** to the project.
> *(Mixes infinitive "to" with gerund "-ing" — neither form is complete.)*
> ✅ The team began **contributing** to the project.
> ✅ The team began **to contribute** to the project.

> ❌ She is dedicated to **study** hard.
> *(After "dedicated to," the "to" is a preposition, not part of an infinitive — needs a gerund.)*
> ✅ She is dedicated to **studying** hard.

### Preposition "To" vs. Infinitive "To"

This is a subtle but important distinction. Sometimes "to" is a **preposition** (followed by a gerund/noun), not part of an infinitive:

| Phrase | "To" is a... | Correct form |
|---|---|---|
| look forward **to** | preposition | *I look forward to **meeting** you.* |
| be used **to** | preposition | *She is used to **working** late.* |
| be dedicated **to** | preposition | *He is dedicated to **improving** the system.* |
| be committed **to** | preposition | *They are committed to **reducing** waste.* |
| object **to** | preposition | *She objected to **being** excluded.* |
| in addition **to** | preposition | *In addition to **teaching**, she mentors.* |

> ❌ I look forward to **see** you.
> ✅ I look forward to **seeing** you.

**💡 Learning Hint:** If you can replace "to" with "toward" and the sentence still makes sense, "to" is a preposition → use a gerund. "I look forward **toward** seeing you" → makes sense → gerund.

## Gerunds and Infinitives in Parallel Structures

When gerunds or infinitives appear in a list, all items must use the same form. See [[Parallelism]] for the full rule.

> ❌ Her hobbies include **painting**, **reading**, and **to cook**.
> ✅ Her hobbies include **painting**, **reading**, and **cooking**.

> ❌ The goal is **to reduce** costs, **to improve** efficiency, and **streamlining** operations.
> ✅ The goal is **to reduce** costs, **to improve** efficiency, and **to streamline** operations.

## Practice Questions

### Question 1

> The researchers avoided **to publish** their findings until they could replicate the results.

A) NO CHANGE
B) publishing
C) to have published
D) publish

**Correct Answer: B**
*Why?* "Avoid" is a gerund-only verb. "Avoided publishing" is correct.

### Question 2

> In addition to **provide** emergency shelter, the organization offers long-term housing assistance.

A) NO CHANGE
B) providing
C) to provide
D) the provision of

**Correct Answer: B**
*Why?* "In addition to" uses "to" as a preposition, not an infinitive marker. Prepositions take gerunds → "providing."
